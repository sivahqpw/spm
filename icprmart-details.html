FORWARD CONTENT REASONING ANALYZER

CONTEXT

Use the following reasoning hierarchy throughout the analysis.

- "Idea" → The author's expressed thought, viewpoint, or mental model.
- "Concept" → The underlying fundamental idea or reality that gives rise to the Idea.
- "Principle" → A universal truth or general principle derived from the Concept.
- "Rule" → A practical guideline or decision rule derived from the Principle.
- "Method" → The specific process, framework, system, or technique used to apply the Rule.
- "Action" → The concrete actions taken by following the Method.
- "Result" → The immediate outcome produced by the Action.
- "Transformation" → The long-term change produced by consistently achieving the right Results.

Important

- Every Idea or Concept is not necessarily true.
- A valid Idea must ultimately be supported by a valid Concept and true Principles.
- If an Idea or Concept is based on a false Principle (an illusion accepted as truth), identify it.
- Distinguish clearly between "Rule" ("What should be done?") and "Method" ("How should it be done?").

REVERSE CONTENT REASONING ANALYZER

CONTEXT

Use the following reasoning hierarchy throughout the analysis.

- "Transformation" → The long-term change produced by consistently achieving the right Results.
- "Result" → The immediate outcome produced by taking the right Actions.
- "Action" → The concrete actions performed.
- "Method" → The process, framework, system, or technique used to perform those Actions.
- "Rule" → The decision guideline that determines the Method.
- "Principle" → The universal truth that justifies the Rule.
- "Concept" → The underlying fundamental idea or reality from which the Principle arises.
- "Idea" → The author's expressed thought or mental model built upon the Concept.

Important

- Every Idea or Concept is not necessarily true.
- A valid Idea must ultimately be supported by a valid Concept and true Principles.
- If the reasoning chain breaks because of a false Principle or unsupported assumption, identify it.
- Distinguish clearly between "Rule" ("What should be done?") and "Method" ("How should it be done?").
